# Bridging the gap between unbiased and biased campaign lift

Currently in the US data is abundant and we’re able to capture a large portion of the population resulting in robust analytical solutions. With increasing restrictions in multiple states beginning from California to Nevada, data capture is decreasing at an alarming rate that’s headed towards a reality where certain portions of population are going to be under or overrepresented leading to biasness. With this increasing biasness of the data, current analytical solutions in the market are prone to also be impacted in accuracy.
 
 	I’m one of the leaders of the data science group that works on Digital Campaign measurement product in the US Retail & CPG domain (https://www.circana.com/solutions/complete-measurement/) which is considered a global standard when dealing with conversion on the offline brick and mortar space. The measurement product captures incremental sales that would have been gained through advertising and which specific marketing tactics are driving it. This would help the brands optimize the campaigns to increase ROAS (Return on Ad spent). The methodology that propels this analysis is built on the concepts of AB testing with a counterfactual approach. Building models on a control group (unexposed to ads), that defines a baseline and then predicts the exposed (to ads) normal behavior if they wouldn’t have been exposed. Any sales delta that is captured between the two scenarios can be attributed to Advertising. With biased datasets where household demographics are disproportionate to the population the control models built are inherently biased themselves, thereby leading to sub-optimal results on the incremental campaign impact.
